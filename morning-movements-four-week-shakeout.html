The four-week shake-out

The FTSE 100 barely moved between late July and late August — about 0.5% from close to close. Underneath that calm surface, names in my coverage universe moved more than thirty percent in both directions. This piece sets out the verified movers and what I take from the dispersion.

26 August 2026 · 2 min read · window: 29 Jul to 24 Aug 2026, closing prices

With the summer results season winding down, I wanted to take stock of the last four weeks rather than a single morning. Between the close on 29 July and the close on 24 August the FTSE 100 moved from 10,908 to 10,854 — a change of about 0.5% lower. The index level tells you very little on its own, though. Underneath it, the dispersion between individual names over those four weeks has been unusually wide, and that is the real story of this piece.

The index in context

The two charts below show the FTSE 100 over the last twelve months and the last six months, with the start of the four-week window marked in gold. The twelve-month picture is one of a market that has ground higher with two visible setbacks along the way; over the last six months the climb has been steadier, and the late-August level sits near the top of the year's range of roughly 9,117 to 10,911.

The four-week risers

Among the companies I track, the standout verified risers over the window were led by the memory and AI-infrastructure names on the US side, with SanDisk Corp up 47.0% and Harworth Group PLC up 39.8%. On the UK side, Greatland Resources Ltd and RWS Holdings PLC both put in strong four-week runs. Every figure in these tables is taken from the same source at both ends of the window — the 29 July and 24 August closing prices — rather than from mixed intraday snapshots.

The four-week fallers

The fallers are led by Vanquis Banking Group PLC, down 31.9% over the four weeks, with GB Group PLC close behind. It is worth saying plainly that a four-week fall of this size is information, not a verdict: some of these are results reactions, and some are sentiment. I have deliberately not attributed a cause to each name here — that is work for the individual company notes, not a movements piece.

The index moved about 0.5% in four weeks; the widest individual moves were more than thirty points of percentage apart. Averages hide almost everything interesting.

What I take from it

Two things. First, dispersion this wide usually means the market is repricing individual business models rather than expressing a single macro view — which suits the way I work, because it is exactly when company-by-company analysis earns its keep. Second, the four-week window is a reminder that a calm index can coexist with violent moves underneath it. I will pick up several of the names above in their own company notes rather than stretch this piece into analysis it has not done.
