Fewer Impairments, Not More Growth

Third quarter trading update, the three months to 31 October 2026. Kingfisher reports in sterling; all per-share figures are in pence and all financial years end 31 January, so “2026/27” is the year to 31 January 2027.

Briefing

  • Thesis: These are a good set of figures, but a big reason for them is a reduction in impairments. Screwfix is working well; B&Q is struggling, and B&Q and Screwfix take up the predominant amount of profitability for the company.
  • The numbers: Second full year upgrade of the year, taking adjusted profit before tax to £625m at the middle of the new £610m–£640m range, up +11.6% year on year. Group sales for the quarter £3,257m, with like-for-like sales up +0.9%. Screwfix like-for-like +6.9% to £765m; B&Q −1.0% to £963m; France −2.8%; Poland +2.1%; Iberia +6.6%.
  • Key strength: Screwfix continues to move forwards at pace — nine-month sales of £2,217m, up +7.1% — and margins look to have continued to stabilise and improve this year.
  • Key risk: Where the B&Q stores are heading. Big-ticket was down −3.7% across the group and fell everywhere except Screwfix and Iberia, and longer-lasting profit growth greater than 3% is going to be a bit of a stretch.
  • Overall stance: Overvalued at this point in time. My valuation comes in at 255.34p against a share price of 382.20p — a −33.2% value opportunity, and a Sell at research grade A+ with 50% probability.

Business Model

Kingfisher is a home improvement retailer with more than 1,800 stores in seven countries. It trades through these banners:

  • B&Q, including TradePoint, in the UK and Ireland
  • Screwfix in the UK and Ireland, with a small Screwfix France business
  • Castorama in France
  • Brico Dépôt in France
  • Castorama in Poland
  • Brico Dépôt in Spain and Portugal

The UK and Ireland still accounts for 78% of group retail profit, using last year's figures.

Third Quarter Trading

Kingfisher have reported their third quarter trading update covering the three months to 31 October 2026. They have made their second full year upgrade, taking adjusted pre-tax profit to £625m at the middle of the new range, up +11.6% year on year.

Group sales for the quarter were £3,257m, with like-for-like sales up +0.9%. Divisionally, it's Screwfix that continues to move forwards at pace, with like-for-like sales up +6.9% to £765m. B&Q saw a drop in sales, −1.0% to £963m.

In France, Castorama saw sales dip −0.5%. Brico Dépôt saw negative sales quite considerably, at −5.3%. France as a total was down −2.8%.

Poland was good however, with sales of £480m after a rise of +2.1%. Iberia was the standout on growth among the European locations, up +6.6%. It has to be remembered that this came from quite a low figure, with sales now £116m.

🎓 Clarity What do “like-for-like” and “constant currency” actually mean? Like-for-like (LFL) sales strip out new stores, closures and disposals, so you are comparing the same shops and websites against the same period a year earlier — it tells you whether the existing estate is trading better or worse, rather than whether the company has simply opened more outlets. Constant currency re-states foreign sales at last year's exchange rates, so a move in the euro or the złoty does not flatter or flatter-free the numbers. It matters here because Kingfisher's story is a mix: Screwfix is adding stores and growing like-for-like, while B&Q's problem is the existing estate. Most of the divisional percentages quoted in this piece are like-for-like, and where the headline reported sales move differs it is shown alongside.

🎓 Clarity Profit before tax, free cash flow, capex, buyback — what are these? Profit before tax (PBT) is what is left of sales after all operating costs and interest, but before the tax bill. Free cash flow is the cash actually generated after running and maintaining the business — the money available for dividends, debt repayment or buybacks. Capital expenditure (capex) is spending on long-lived assets such as new stores, refits and IT; guidance of around £390m tells you how much cash is going back into the estate. A share buyback is the company purchasing its own shares and cancelling them, which shrinks the share count so each remaining share owns a larger slice of the profit — that is why the share count used for earnings per share falls this year.

Marketplace

On the B&Q side, the marketplace is still moving forwards, with the value of goods sold through it up +33% to £143m in the quarter.

The company only takes a percentage of the gross merchandise sales on the platform, a commission of around 10% to 15%. On £143m that is roughly £14m to £21m for the quarter, against B&Q sales of £963m. B&Q sales including the marketplace only rose by +1.4%.

The company talks of what happens “as marketplace continues to scale”. I think this is an area that needs to move at greater pace if they actually want it to work out.

Across the group, marketplace gross merchandise value was £170m, up +36%.

Like-for-like sales have improved each quarter this year, from −4.1% to −1.8% to −1.0%, as weather and footfall returned to normal.

  • Big-ticket was down −6.3%, with bathrooms underperforming and kitchens partly making up for it.
  • E-commerce sales grew +16%. TradePoint like-for-like sales were up +1.6%.

Screwfix

Total sales rose +7.4% to £765m, with like-for-like sales up +6.9% on volume and customer growth.

  • Core categories, around 85% of Screwfix sales, grew +6.8%, and seasonal +11.3%.
  • 6 stores opened in the quarter, including 4 City stores.

Castorama France

Like-for-like sales were −0.5%, or +0.2% including the marketplace, in a market the company says fell by low single digits.

  • Core categories were flat at −0.1% and big-ticket was −1.8%, improving after the launch of a new kitchen range.
  • E-commerce sales grew +15%. A third store is being transferred to a franchisee, and 4 store revamps are due to complete in the first half of 2027.

Brico Dépôt France

Like-for-like sales were −5.3%, after −5.4% in the second quarter.

  • The company puts this down to weaker demand in building materials and larger project categories, which Brico Dépôt is weighted towards. Core was −5.2% and big-ticket −8.2%.
  • Trade sales grew +6%. Website traffic is still affected following the earlier problems with the site.

Poland

Sales were £480m, up +3.9% at constant exchange rates and +2.1% like-for-like, in a market growing by low single digits.

  • Core was up +3.4% and seasonal +2.0%. Big-ticket was −3.4% on weak bathroom sales.
  • E-commerce sales grew +24% and trade customers are now 28% of sales. 1 store opened in the quarter.

Iberia

Sales were £116m, up +11.6% at constant exchange rates and +6.6% like-for-like, led by core and seasonal categories.

  • Trade sales grew +24%. The 2 stores opened in the first half are trading ahead of the company's expectations.

🎓 Clarity What is an “adjusting item”, and why does it change the verdict? Adjusting items are costs or gains a company strips out of its headline profit because it considers them one-off or not representative — here, mostly impairments. An impairment is a write-down of the carrying value of an asset, either a store or goodwill (the premium paid in a past acquisition), when the company accepts it will not earn back what the books say it is worth. Adjusted profit before tax excludes them; statutory profit before tax includes them. At Kingfisher the gap has been large and recurring — −£93m, −£221m, −£182m, an average of −£165m a year — so treating them as one-offs flatters the picture. That is exactly why these are a good set of figures but a big reason for them is a reduction in impairments, and why the valuation below uses the earnings figure after adjusting items rather than the adjusted one.

🎓 Clarity Why does the valuation use 23.4p when the company's own measure is 28.0p — and what does “override” mean? Earnings per share (EPS) is profit after tax divided by the number of shares — the profit attributable to each individual share. There are two versions here: 28.0p on the adjusted basis, which excludes the write-downs, and 23.4p after my forecast adjusting items, which includes them. The valuation below uses 23.4p, the lower, all-in figure, because the adjustments have recurred every year. “Override” simply means the EPS input in the model is my own forecast for the year to 31 January 2027 rather than a figure already reported by the company — that is why it is labelled (override, FY2026/27e) in the snapshot. Every number that flows from it — the price-to-earnings ratio of 16.3, the 255.34p valuation and the −33.2% value opportunity — is built on 23.4p. Using 28.0p instead would produce a higher valuation; I have deliberately not done that.

Clarity

Ultimately, with Kingfisher, I have in the past and continue to have some concern about where their B&Q stores are heading. They seem to struggle on their sales front.

While last year saw a good uptick in earnings per share, revenues have been patchy over the last couple of years.

2026 was the only one of the last four years in which sales increased as a group, and it was only the second time in five years that the company managed to improve earnings per share.

I expect, based upon this guidance, that margins have continued to stabilise and improve for this year.

Screwfix is working well for them, and they can see growth there. B&Q is struggling, and B&Q and Screwfix take up the predominant amount of profitability for the company.

They are seeing positive movements in their Spanish division, but it's very small in comparison to the rest of the operation.

These are a good set of figures, but a big reason for them is a reduction in impairments.

Valuation

I have used my calculated earnings per share figure of 23.4p for the 2026/27 full year.

I'm assuming a growth factor of 3% going forwards. The company is making good progress on its sales and moving ahead, but I think there are issues with what they're selling and how they're selling it.

I think they're going to be struggling, and longer-lasting profit growth greater than 3% is going to be a bit of a stretch.

That being said, because I think they have a few items that are going to run down slowly over time, I am giving them a 10% valuation uplift to compensate for that.

My valuation comes in at 255.34p per share, which, given that the share price currently sits at 382.20p, gives a −33.2% value opportunity.

Overall, I think that they are overvalued at this point in time.

Risks and What Could Go Wrong (for the Sell case)

The Sell rests on my 23.4p earnings figure, a 3% growth factor and an 8x multiple. Here is the case against me, drawn from the same update:

  • The upgrades keep coming: This is the second full year upgrade of the year. The bottom of the adjusted profit before tax range has risen by £45m since March and the top by £15m, and £625m is already up +11.6% year on year. A third upgrade would make 3% growth look conservative.
  • Screwfix is accelerating, not just growing: Like-for-like sales have gone +3.3%, +3.5%, +4.1%, +7.1%, +6.9% over the last five quarters, with 6 more stores opened in the quarter. Screwfix is one of the two businesses that take up the predominant amount of profitability.
  • B&Q is improving: Like-for-like sales have improved each quarter this year, from −4.1% to −1.8% to −1.0%, e-commerce grew +16%, and the company says B&Q gained market share in the quarter. If that turns positive, the drag I am worried about reverses.
  • Impairments might genuinely be ending: My forecast takes adjusting items to only −£76m, 12.2% of the adjusted figure, against 32.5% last year. There is only £67m of Castorama France goodwill left to write off at all. Once it has gone, statutory and adjusted earnings converge upwards.
  • Big-ticket is cyclical, not structural: Big-ticket is around 15% of group sales and was down −3.7%, in markets the company says declined by low single digits in the UK, Ireland and France. A rate-driven recovery in kitchens and bathrooms would lift the weakest line.
  • The share count is shrinking: £167m of the £300m buyback was done by 31 October, with free cash flow guided at £490m to £520m. Fewer shares means higher earnings per share on the same profit, and there is a 3.2% dividend yield to be paid while waiting.
  • The small growers scale up: Iberia is up +11.6% at constant currency with the 2 new stores trading ahead of expectations, Poland is up +3.9%, and group marketplace gross merchandise value rose +36% to £170m. My view is that marketplace needs to move at greater pace — if it does, I am wrong on the pace.

What Would Change My Mind

  • B&Q back to sustained positive like-for-like: Not one improving quarter from a low base, but positive prints that hold. That is the concern about where their stores are heading, and it is the cheapest thing for them to fix.
  • Big-ticket turning across the group: It fell everywhere except Screwfix and Iberia. Kitchens, bathrooms and storage returning to growth would change the quality of the sales line, not just the headline.
  • Impairments actually stopping: Three years of −£93m, −£221m and −£182m is not a one-off pattern. A clean year end with no fresh Castorama France goodwill write-down, and my 23.4p moving towards the 28.0p adjusted figure, would re-rate my own input.
  • Evidence of growth above 3% lasting: I think longer-lasting profit growth greater than 3% is a stretch. Two or three years of it would justify a higher growth factor, and the formula is sensitive to that assumption.
  • Marketplace moving at greater pace: £143m of gross merchandise value at a 10% to 15% commission is roughly £14m to £21m against B&Q sales of £963m. Materially faster scaling, and a bigger take, would make it a real profit line rather than a talking point.
  • The price doing the work: At 255.34p the value opportunity closes on its own. A share price falling towards my valuation turns this from a Sell into something I would look at again.

Bottom Line — Sell

Kingfisher have upgraded twice this year and taken adjusted profit before tax to £625m at the middle of the new range, up +11.6%. Screwfix continues to move forwards at pace. But B&Q is struggling, France is going backwards at −2.8%, big-ticket fell −3.7% across the group, and a big reason for this year's profit step-up is a reduction in impairments rather than a step-up in trading.

On my calculated earnings per share of 23.4p, a 3% growth factor, an 8x multiple and a 10% uplift for the items I think will run down slowly, the valuation is 255.34p. The share price sits at 382.20p, which prices in more than I think they can deliver. Overall, I think that they are overvalued at this point in time.

Sell. Value opportunity: -33.2%. Probability: 50%. Research grade: A+.
