Briefing

  • Thesis: The commercial bank is doing a lot of the work, the retail bank is earning more before credit costs, earnings per share have been compounding hard from both profit growth and buybacks, and the shares are still cheap on those earnings.
  • The numbers: Nine-month operating profit £6,543m, up +13.4%; earnings per share 57.4p, up +13.2%; rolling four-quarter earnings per share 74.7p. On my forward earnings per share of 77.19p, a 4% growth rate and a multiple of 9, less a -10% reduction, the valuation is 850.33p against 591.90p — a +43.7% value opportunity.
  • Key strength: Commercial & Institutional, which lifted nine-month operating profit by +£544m to £3,569m and contributed 70% of the net group profit increase, with a rising net interest margin behind it.
  • Key risk: Impairments. Nine months of 2026 at £700m is already above the whole of 2025, and the third quarter loan impairment rate of 24 basis points is close to the company's "below 25 basis points" guidance.
  • Overall stance: Strong Buy, research grade A, risk factor 24%, probability 50%. Most of what was likely to go badly wrong has already gone wrong.

Business Model

NatWest is a UK bank reporting through three divisions, with central items and other sitting alongside them to reconcile the divisions to the group.

  • Retail Banking: personal customers in the UK. Lending is mostly mortgages, alongside £8.5bn of credit cards and £11.7bn of other personal lending. It takes current account and savings deposits, including through NatWest Boxed.
  • Private Banking & Wealth Management: wealthier individuals and their businesses, through Coutts, NatWest's premier banking, and now Evelyn Partners. It earns from lending and deposits as well as fees on assets under management.
  • Commercial & Institutional: businesses from small and medium-sized enterprises through to mid-sized and large corporates and financial institutions. It lends, takes business deposits, and runs payments, currencies and capital markets through NatWest Markets.

Impairments

🎓 Clarity What is an impairment charge, and what does "Stage 3" mean? An impairment charge is the amount a bank sets aside in the profit and loss account for loans it now expects not to be repaid in full — it is a cost, taken before the money is actually lost. Banks sort loans into three stages: Stage 1 is performing, Stage 2 is where credit risk has deteriorated, and Stage 3 is credit-impaired, which usually means the borrower has defaulted or is close to it. "Rising Stage 3 flows" means more loans are tipping into that worst bucket, which forces bigger provisions. That matters here because impairments are the one line on this result moving the wrong way, and the loan impairment rate — provisions as a percentage of gross customer loans — is the number bumping up against the company's own guidance ceiling.

Base Rates and the Structural Hedge

The company's outlook assumes the Bank of England base rate rises by 25 basis points to 4% in the fourth quarter of 2026.

My expectation is that base rates will rise, but not that dramatically. I could see them 1% to 1.5% higher than they currently are over the next 12 to 18 months, with around 1% to 1.25% higher in 12 months' time.

The impact of this rolls through slowly. As positions in the structural hedge mature, they can be replaced at higher yields, which slowly lifts the net interest margin as the sources of lending get replaced. The company already expected its hedge yield to rise from 2.4% in 2025 to around 3.1% in 2026 before any further rate rises.

🎓 Clarity What is a "structural hedge", and why does it make rate rises slow-acting? A large slice of a bank's deposits sit in current accounts and instant-access savings that pay little or nothing and do not move much when rates change. To earn a predictable return on that money, the bank invests it in a rolling ladder of fixed-rate instruments — typically spread over about five years. That ladder is the structural hedge. Because only a fraction matures each year, a rise in market rates is picked up gradually, as maturing tranches are reinvested at today's higher yields. That is why a rate rise today shows up as a slowly rising net interest margin over several years rather than an immediate jump — and why I expect the benefit to be larger in years two and three than in year one.

The company discloses the one-year sensitivity of its net interest earnings to a parallel 25 basis point upward shift in all yield curves at +£120m at 30 June 2026, down from £194m at the end of 2025.

Valuation

I anticipate general profitability to increase by approximately 4% over the next 12 months, to £8,227.44m before tax.

Because I think interest rates are likely to rise by as much as 1.25% over that period, I am also anticipating an additional £600m or so of pre-tax profit from the widening of the net interest margin, taking pre-tax profit to £8,827.44m.

As with all UK banks, NatWest pays a higher rate of tax because of the bank surcharge. Through 2027 into 2028 the combined rate will be 31%.

I'm going to continue using a growth rate of 4% because of the size of the company, with a valuation multiple of 9.

It's a forward earnings per share estimate for the next 12 months, so the time it will take to get there isn't that long. However, I'm reducing my valuation by -10% because of the time it's going to take to get there.

I have a lot of faith in where the company is currently heading, and there are tailwinds that will aid it. It has already been through a period where spending and loan demand haven't been especially good for banks, so I don't see the situation weakening on a demand basis.

I do see net interest margins improving, and the company making forward progress, especially on the commercial side of the business, in the coming 12 months.

My valuation comes in at 850.33p per share, which, at the current share price of 591.90p, gives a +43.7% value opportunity.

I give it a strong buy, with a relatively low risk factor of 24%. Most of the things that were likely to go badly wrong for the company have already gone wrong, such as higher taxes on profitability, and I don't see impairments becoming outsized compared to where they are currently.

On the rolling earnings per share of 74.7p, and without my reduction, the valuation would be 914.33p, a +54.5% value opportunity. I am not using this figure, as it's higher than my forward earnings forecast, but it's further backing that the company is currently undervalued.

Risks and What Could Go Wrong

  • Impairments rising faster than expected: the nine months at £700m is already above the whole of 2025, and the third quarter loan impairment rate of 24 basis points is close to the "below 25 basis points" guidance. I do have some concern about impairments, and their rate of rise.
  • Retail credit quality: Retail Banking's third quarter charge of £192m was its highest in the series, against £96m in the second quarter, driven by rising Stage 3 flows as credit card cohorts written since 2022 mature.
  • Rate rises cutting both ways: mortgage customers refinancing off fixed rates, unsecured borrowers, small and medium-sized businesses on variable rates, and commercial property all feel higher rates on the credit side, even as the margin benefits.
  • Deposit competition: some of the net interest margin benefit would be given back if deposit rates have to rise to keep customers.
  • Concentration in one division: Commercial & Institutional contributed 70% of the net group profit increase, and almost all of its own increase is net interest income, with markets trading income already lower.
  • Tax: the bank surcharge takes the combined rate to 31% from 1 January 2027 to 31 March 2028, which is already in my 77.19p forward earnings figure.
  • Liquidity drift: the average liquidity coverage ratio has slipped from 147% at the end of 2025 to 137% at 30 September 2026 — not a concern for me at this point in time, but it is a trend.
  • Macro base case: the group's own half-year base case already had inflation peaking at around 4%, real incomes under pressure and growth slowing to 1.0%.

What Would Change My Mind

  • The loan impairment rate breaching guidance: a fourth quarter above 25 basis points, or a full-year rate materially above 21 basis points, would make impairments outsized compared to where they are currently — which is the assumption my low 24% risk factor rests on.
  • Retail credit costs compounding: another step up from the £192m third quarter charge, with Stage 3 flows broadening beyond the post-2022 credit card cohorts.
  • Commercial & Institutional stalling: net interest income flattening off from £1,762m a quarter, or costs breaking out of the flat £3,266m nine-month run rate.
  • Rates not rising: if base rates stay put rather than moving 1% to 1.25% higher, the £600m of extra pre-tax profit in my forecast does not arrive, and the 77.19p drops back towards the £8,227.44m base case.
  • Guidance being cut: any walk-back on 2026 income of around £17.9bn, return on tangible equity above 19%, or the 2028 targets.
  • Buybacks stopping: the share count falling from 10,467m to 7,931m has been half the earnings per share story. No buyback announced with the full year 2026 results would take that engine out.

Bottom Line — Strong Buy

The commercial side of the business is growing very strongly and the retail side is growing quite well, the net interest margin and return on tangible equity are both up, and Evelyn Partners has broadened the earnings base. Earnings per share have compounded from both profit growth and a significant reduction in shares in issue, and the shares are still cheap on those earnings with a 5.5% yield.

Impairments are the big question mark over the coming period, and I do have some concern about their rate of rise — but most of the things that were likely to go badly wrong have already gone wrong, including higher taxes on profitability, and I don't see impairments becoming outsized compared to where they are currently.

On forward earnings of 77.19p, a 4% growth rate and a multiple of 9, less the -10% reduction for the time it takes to get there, I value the shares at 850.33p against 591.90p.

Strong Buy. Value opportunity: +43.7%. Probability: 50%. Research grade: A.
