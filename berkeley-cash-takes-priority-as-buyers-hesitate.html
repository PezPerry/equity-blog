Berkeley: cash takes priority as buyers hesitate

Berkeley's latest update reinforces my caution about the housing outlook. Management says prolonged conflict in the Middle East and political uncertainty in the UK are affecting sentiment and trading. Enquiries remain good and stable, but turning that interest into committed purchases is the issue.

Buyers are waiting

Berkeley describes customers without an immediate need to move and readily available liquidity as “more cautious to commit”. Interest in its homes is taking longer to become commitment. Management says this position is similar to June, and warns that some transactions may wait until after the Budget and election uncertainty dissipates.

Slower commitments can affect construction timing and the cash tied up in developments. I see little here to suggest a quick recovery in buying confidence.

The 25% reduction needs context

The decision to re-phase delivery dates from 1 April. September's update describes production across the four-year period as around 25% lower, with:

“our focus on cash generation, ahead of short-term profit targets”

Berkeley trading update · 11 September 2026

The chart shows four annual delivery figures. I could not verify a consistent quarterly series in Berkeley's published results.

The separate illustration shows the scale of the production decision. The statement does not provide the original planned unit total or a quarterly delivery schedule. Applying 25% to last year's completions would therefore create a forecast Berkeley has not given.

Cash comes first, profit plan maintained

Berkeley continues to operate within its £1.4bn pre-tax profit plan for FY27–FY30. The profile is expected to be broadly even, with demand affecting timing. Current-year profit is expected to be slightly weighted towards the first half, subject to completions.

The company returned £60m through buybacks in the first four months. It has repurchased £171m towards the £640m shareholder-return target covering October 2025 to September 2030. Half-year net cash is expected to be around £250m, after shareholder returns and investment in the core business and Build to Rent.

My focus is whether lower investment commitments translate into stronger cash generation, which management expects from the end of FY27 onwards. That may protect flexibility while demand is weak. I would still want to see firmer buying decisions before becoming more positive about the near-term outlook.
