Crest Nicholson: worse figures, same asset case

This morning's trading update from Crest Nicholson is, on the whole, more negative news: profit guidance cut again, the sales rate lower, and the covenant agreement taking longer than it should. The one genuine positive is net debt, which will not be as bad as guided because the land disposals they warned would be difficult have actually happened. None of it changes the asset case I set out in July.

Crest Nicholson put out a trading update at 7am today. Taken together it is more negative news for a company that has had little else this year. The one positive is on the balance sheet: year-end net debt is now guided to £70m to £90m, against the previous £100m to £120m, an improvement of about £30m. That is because they have managed to complete land disposals, which they had previously said would be quite difficult in this marketplace, together with a further fire-remediation recovery from a third party. That is a real positive and should not be lost in the rest.

What the update says

The rest of the statement is the concerning part. Market conditions were more subdued than expected through the summer. The net open-market sales rate over the last six weeks was 0.35 per outlet per week, against 0.48 in the first half and 0.55 in the same period last year. Full-year completions are now guided to 1,350 to 1,400 (previously 1,400 to 1,500), and full-year EBIT is now expected to be a loss of around £10m, where the previous guidance was a profit of £5m to £10m. Bulk sales remain under pricing pressure and there are further net realisable value provisions on a small number of sites.

Then the lenders. The company says it remains in constructive discussions to amend its covenants, but now anticipates some slippage in the current timetable and will update the market in due course. As always, it is concerning how long an agreement on a covenant is taking. The interest-cover waiver was granted so that the test would be measured at 31 August and reported by 30 September; a slipping timetable against that backdrop is not what anyone holding the shares wanted to read.

The share price

The share price is at a very low level. It closed at 61.3p on 2 September, down about 57% on the year from 143.5p at the end of December, and against a twelve-month high of 175.3p last October. That close is also exactly the twelve-month low, first touched on 27 April. As always, a concerning matter is that anyone who has ever bought Crest Nicholson shares is now sitting at a loss, or next to one, at these levels. Why I am not changing the valuation

Nonetheless, I am sticking with the guidance and valuation from my last write-up on 16 July. Crest Nicholson has significant assets, and it should not be valued on the profit, or the actual loss, it might generate in this twelve-month period. It should be valued on the assets it has available. In July I took the stated net asset value of £688.8m, discounted it by a deliberately huge 40%, and arrived at a discounted asset value of £413.28m, or 160.97p per share.

The market capitalisation today stands at £157.4m. Set that against £413.28m of discounted assets and the valuation sits 162.6% above the current share price, up from the +140.3% I recorded in July simply because the price has fallen further since then.

What I take from it

As a side note, that is the thought this update leaves me with. At £157.4m of market value against a land bank and work in progress that I have already discounted by 40% to reach £413.28m, a cash-rich peer could pay a substantial premium to the current price and still be buying the assets cheaply. I still very much think there is value in this company. We may be in for a rougher few days on these figures, and the covenant delay will keep the shares trading on fear rather than fundamentals until it is signed. Ultimately, though, there is significant value here, and nothing in today's statement changes where that value sits.
