How to Read This Report

This is a business-understanding pack on Keller Group plc, prepared to a research cut-off of 19 August 2026, 20:48 (BST). Its purpose is to explain how the company earns money and what drives that outcome — not to value the equity. No rating, price target or valuation multiple is produced anywhere in this document.

Three evidence tiers are used throughout and kept visibly separate:

  • Verified fact — a figure or statement taken directly from an official issuer or regulatory document that was opened and read. Every such figure carries a unit, currency, period or as-at date, and an identified source.
  • Derived figure — arithmetic performed here on sourced inputs. Every derivation is shown with its formula in ## Calculation Notes.
  • Inference / unknown — reasoned judgement, or an explicit statement that something was not obtained. Inferences are labelled in the text.

Basis conventions that matter for Keller and are applied consistently below:

  • Underlying vs statutory. Keller reports both. "Underlying operating profit" excludes non-underlying items (amortisation of acquired intangibles, restructuring, ERP programme costs, certain fair-value movements). The two are never mixed in a single series here.
  • Actual vs constant currency (CER). Keller quotes many growth rates at constant currency. Where a percentage is company-stated at CER it is labelled; where a percentage is computed from reported sterling figures it is labelled as at actual rates. These are not interchangeable — Keller earns roughly six-tenths of revenue in North America, so translation effects are large.
  • Net debt basis. Keller quotes covenant/IAS 17 net debt (pre-IFRS 16 lease liabilities). All net debt and leverage figures below are on that basis.
  • Segment basis. The Group reported North America / Europe / AMEA through FY2023 and North America / Europe and Middle East / Asia-Pacific from the FY2024 cycle onwards. Segment series are therefore not continuous across the five years, and no pre-FY2024 segment figure is compared with a post-FY2023 one.

Where a source could not be opened, or a number could not be proved, this report says so rather than filling the gap.

Executive Briefing

Keller is the world's largest geotechnical specialist contractor: it builds the ground-engineering works that sit beneath other people's structures — piling, ground improvement, grouting, earth retention and, in North America, manufactured post-tension products. It is a high-volume, short-cycle subcontractor, not a main contractor. In FY2025 it executed c.5,500 projects, of which 43% had a contract value between £25,000 and £250,000, its largest customer was under 4% of Group revenue and its top ten customers were 15% (source: FY2025 preliminary results, 3 March 2026). That granularity is the single most important structural fact about the business: it means order-book concentration risk is low, but it also means the order book converts fast and gives only about half a year of forward revenue cover.

The five-year record is one of a materially repaired margin rather than a materially larger company. On a consistent underlying basis, revenue rose from £2,222.5m in FY2021 (restated) to £3,087.3m in FY2025, a +£864.8m / +38.9% change (8.6% CAGR, derived). Underlying operating profit rose from £88.5m to £218.2m, +£129.7m / +146.6% (25.3% CAGR, derived), lifting the underlying operating margin from 3.98% to 7.07% — roughly +310bps (derived). Return on capital employed moved from 13.9% to 30.7%, and the balance sheet swung from £119.4m net debt to £59.7m net cash, a £179.1m improvement (derived). The improvement is real, primary-sourced and reconciled across five separate regulated announcements.

The FY2025 result, however, shows the profit engine changing hands. North America underlying operating profit fell 9.6% at constant currency to £166.2m (FY2024: £190.0m) as Suncoast's residential end-market weakened and the Foundations business normalised after a buoyant 2024, while Europe and Middle East underlying operating profit rose from £7.9m to £38.8m on the non-recurrence of a loss-making Middle East project and operational improvement. Group underlying operating profit still advanced, from £212.6m to £218.2m (+2.6% at actual rates), with the margin flat at 7.1%.

On the owner's specific arithmetic check: North America contributed 76.2% of Group underlying operating profit in FY2025 (£166.2m ÷ £218.2m) and 89.4% in FY2024 (£190.0m ÷ £212.6m). The supplied check value of 78.2% for FY2024 is not supported by any primary source opened here and is not used. Because unallocated central costs are deducted after the divisions, North America's share of the divisional profit pool is lower — 70.5% in FY2025 and 83.8% in FY2024 — and both bases are shown wherever the claim appears.

Trading in 2026 has been materially stronger than 2025. H1 2026 revenue was £1,608.0m (H1 2025: £1,457.7m, +10.3% at actual rates) and underlying operating profit £117.9m (H1 2025: £102.6m, +14.9% at actual rates), with underlying diluted EPS +22.4% to 120.1p — the gap between profit and EPS growth reflecting share count reduction and below-the-line effects. The order book reached a record c.£1.9bn at 30 June 2026 (30 June 2025: c.£1.6bn), lifted by a $207m variation on the I-40 highway reconstruction announced on 9 June 2026 that took Keller's secured work on that single project to c.$380m. On 7 July 2026 the Board said FY2026 revenue and underlying operating profit would be "materially ahead of current market consensus" of £3,150m and £223m; by the 4 August interim the company-compiled consensus had moved to £3,337m revenue and £242m underlying operating profit.

Two things deserve the reader's scepticism. First, cash conversion has weakened as growth has resumed: H1 2026 free cash flow was £16.4m on £117.9m of underlying operating profit, and company-defined free cash conversion fell to 36% (H1 2025: 50%) — normal for a working-capital-hungry contractor in a volume upswing, but it is the mechanism by which a record order book can become a cash problem. Second, the record order book is less diversified than it looks: North America's order book rose from £1,026.3m to £1,367.9m over the twelve months to 30 June 2026, and management has attributed the record level substantially to one multi-year highway contract.

Business Model

What Keller sells. Keller is a specialist subcontractor in ground engineering. Its work is the part of a construction project that happens before the visible structure exists: driving and boring piles, improving weak or variable soils so they can carry load, injecting grout to stabilise ground, retaining earth during excavation, and providing instrumentation and monitoring. It also manufactures and installs post-tension systems for concrete slabs in North America through Suncoast, which is a product-plus-installation business rather than a pure contracting business — a distinction that matters because its economics track US residential housing starts rather than the commercial construction cycle.

Who pays. Keller's direct customers are overwhelmingly main contractors, developers and public infrastructure clients. The FY2025 preliminary results disclose that the largest customer was less than 4% of Group revenue and the top ten customers 15%. There is therefore no meaningful single-customer dependency at Group level. The counterpart to that is that Keller sits one layer down the contractual chain from the ultimate client on most work, which affects payment timing and dispute exposure.

Contract shape. The FY2025 announcement states Keller worked on c.5,500 projects in the year, 43% of which had a value between £25,000 and £250,000. The typical job is small and short. That produces three consequences that run through the rest of this report:

1. Revenue visibility is short. A year-end order book of roughly £1.5bn against roughly £3.1bn of revenue is about half a year of cover (derived: £1,541.7m ÷ £3,087.3m = 0.50x). 2. Pricing is repriced quickly. Management stated at the 20 May 2026 AGM update that it continues to capture cost increases through pricing on new contracts and existing mechanisms — feasible precisely because the book turns over fast. 3. Large contracts are the exception and are announced. The I-40 highway variation of 9 June 2026 ($207m, taking Keller's secured I-40 work to c.$380m over the next two to three years) was material enough to warrant its own RNS and to move the Group order book to a record c.£1.9bn.

Where the revenue arises. In FY2025, North America was 58.8% of Group revenue, Europe and Middle East 28.3% and Asia-Pacific 12.9% (derived from the FY2025 divisional table). In H1 2026 the North American share rose to 61.2%, consistent with the company's own 7 July 2026 statement that North America is "around 60% of Group revenue".

Demand drivers, as the company frames them. The FY2025 announcement cites infrastructure investment, population growth, energy transition, climate resilience and technology adoption. Two of these are visible in the 2025–26 evidence rather than merely asserted: data centre construction and large infrastructure projects including New York's Hudson Tunnel were named as the drivers of North American revenue growth in FY2025, and the 7 July 2026 update again cited infrastructure and data centre demand offsetting a softening South Florida residential market.

Operating leverage

Keller's margin history is the clearest evidence of how the economics work. Between FY2021 and FY2025, revenue grew 38.9% while underlying operating profit grew 146.6%. That is not conventional fixed-cost leverage on volume alone — FY2022 revenue jumped 32.5% year on year while margin fell 30bps to 3.7%, because inflation, supply chain disruption and operational problems in North America Foundations absorbed the volume benefit. The margin repair came in FY2023–FY2024 (6.1% then 7.1%) from pricing discipline, contract selection and the non-recurrence of loss-making work, not from scale.

The practical reading is that Keller's operating leverage is price- and execution-led, not volume-led. A single badly-priced or badly-executed region can erase a year of margin progress: Europe's underlying operating profit collapsed to £1.8m in FY2023 (from £29.1m in FY2022) on Nordic project performance, and Europe and Middle East earned only £7.9m in FY2024 on £835.1m of revenue — a 0.95% margin — before recovering to £38.8m in FY2025.

Cyclicality and end-market mix

Keller's cyclicality is not uniform. Three distinct cycles operate inside one company:

  • US residential (Suncoast). Directly geared to housing starts. The FY2025 announcement attributes Suncoast's decline to "an unfavourable interest rate environment and higher housing prices, driving a significant reduction in housing starts". This is the most rate-sensitive part of the Group.
  • US non-residential and infrastructure. Data centres, tunnels and highways. This is currently the growth engine and is contract- and programme-driven rather than rate-driven in the short term.
  • Europe/Middle East and Asia-Pacific construction. Regionally idiosyncratic — the 7 July 2026 update described Scandinavia, central Europe and the Middle East as strong and western Europe as subdued, with Australian foundations facing pricing pressure while Austral held its 2025 momentum.

This mix is the company's stated defence: geographic diversity and "sector agility". The FY2025 outcome supports the claim in a limited way — Europe and Middle East plus Asia-Pacific added a combined £32.8m of underlying operating profit year on year, more than offsetting North America's £23.8m decline (both derived at actual rates), so Group profit still rose.

Capital intensity

Keller is moderately capital-intensive by contracting standards because it owns rigs and plant. FY2025 net capital expenditure was £77.5m on £3,087.3m of revenue (2.51%, derived), up from £60.0m on £2,986.7m (2.01%, derived) in FY2024. Implied depreciation and amortisation within underlying profit was £109.1m in FY2025 and £108.8m in FY2024 (derived as underlying EBITDA less underlying operating profit), so FY2025 net capex ran at roughly 0.71x that charge. That ratio should be read with care: the D&A figure includes IFRS 16 right-of-use asset depreciation, which has no corresponding cash capex line, so the underlying fleet reinvestment rate is higher than 0.71x. The direction — capex rising 29.2% year on year as volumes recover — is unambiguous and is one route by which free cash flow tightened in 2025 and H1 2026.

Competitive position

Keller describes itself in every announcement examined as "the world's largest geotechnical specialist contractor". Nothing in the primary evidence set independently ranks Keller against named competitors, so market share is treated here as not verified. What is verified and economically meaningful is the fragmentation of Keller's own revenue (5,500 projects, 43% between £25k and £250k, no customer above 4%), which is consistent with a market where scale is expressed through geographic and technique breadth and rig fleet, rather than through dominance of large contracts. The I-40 award is notable precisely because it is atypical.

Risks, Unknowns and What to Watch

Risks evidenced in the primary record

1. Concentration of Group profit in North America. North America generated 76.2% of Group underlying operating profit in FY2025 and 89.4% in FY2024 (derived), and 91.4% of the twelve-month order-book increase to 30 June 2026 (derived). A US construction slowdown is not diversifiable within Keller's current mix, notwithstanding the geographic spread of revenue. 2. Single-contract dependence has increased. The I-40 project represents c.$380m of secured work with c.$310m still to run over two to three years. Keller's historic strength is that no job matters much; this one does. No margin disclosure for it was obtained. 3. Cash conversion under growth. Company-defined free cash conversion fell from 50% to 36% year on year in the first half; free cash flow was £16.4m against £117.9m of underlying operating profit. 4. Execution risk is proven, not theoretical. Europe's underlying operating profit fell to £1.8m in FY2023 on Nordic project performance and Europe and Middle East earned only £7.9m in FY2024 on a Middle East project loss. A single region can and has destroyed a year of Group margin progress. 5. Control environment history. The Austral financial reporting fraud restated FY2021 and prior-year profits by £4.3m and £6.7m respectively. The company reported no cash leakage and described it as isolated and contained, and Austral is currently cited as a positive contributor. The event nonetheless sits in the five-year record. 6. Residential rate sensitivity. Suncoast's FY2025 decline is explicitly attributed to interest rates, house prices and reduced housing starts. 7. Asia-Pacific margin erosion. H1 2026 margin fell 159bps on 25.3% revenue growth (both derived) — the classic signature of buying volume. 8. Translation exposure. With roughly 60% of revenue in North America, reported sterling growth diverged from constant-currency growth by 3.3 percentage points on FY2025 North American revenue and by roughly 7 percentage points on the North American order book at closing rates (derived).

Discrepancy Register

D1 — FY2021 restatement following the Austral financial reporting fraud (resolved; restated basis used). Original FY2021 readings (8 March 2022): revenue £2,224.4m, underlying EBITDA £190.2m, underlying operating profit £92.8m, underlying diluted EPS 88.4p, statutory operating profit £80.5m, statutory diluted EPS 86.1p, ROCE 14.4%, AMEA operating profit £3.4m. Restated readings (7 March 2023): revenue £2,222.5m, underlying EBITDA £185.9m, underlying operating profit £88.5m, underlying diluted EPS 84.2p, statutory operating profit £76.4m, statutory diluted EPS 77.2p, ROCE 13.9%, AMEA operating profit £(0.9)m. Reason: overstatement of Austral's performance from 2019 onwards by two senior finance individuals; disclosed FY2021 operating profit impact £4.3m; no cash leakage (net debt unchanged at £119.4m). Resolution: the restated series is used throughout; the original readings are preserved here only.

D2 — North America share of Group underlying operating profit: supplied check value not supported (resolved). An untrusted external check value of 78.2% for FY2024 was supplied for verification. Recomputation from primary inputs gives £190.0m ÷ £212.6m = 89.37%, i.e. 89.4%, and £166.2m ÷ £218.2m = 76.17%, i.e. 76.2% for FY2025. Both supplied input pairs were confirmed against the FY2025 preliminary results announcement. Resolution: 89.4% (FY2024) and 76.2% (FY2025) are used consistently; 78.2% is rejected and appears nowhere outside this register.

D3 — H1 2025 comparatives quoted on two bases (resolved as a basis difference, both preserved). The issuer's H1 2026 results page states revenue growth of +11.1% and underlying operating profit growth of +17.1%, implying rebased H1 2025 comparatives of approximately £1,449m and £100.6m. The full announcement text and the H1 2025 announcement itself give as-reported H1 2025 figures of £1,457.7m and £102.6m, implying growth of +10.3% and +14.9%. Resolution: the higher growth rates are constant-currency; the lower are at actual rates. As-reported figures are used for all levels and derived percentages in this report, with CER rates quoted only as issuer statements. Both readings preserved.

D4 — FY2024 Group order book: £1.6bn versus a £1,500m rendering (resolved). The FY2024 preliminary results state a record year-end order book of £1.6bn (2023: £1.5bn). One retrieval of the FY2025 preliminary results highlights table rendered the order book as £1,500m for both 2025 and 2024. Resolution: the divisional order books disclosed in the FY2025 announcement sum to £1,610.0m at 31 December 2024 and £1,541.7m at 31 December 2025, which reconciles to the issuer's rounded £1.6bn and £1.5bn descriptions and shows the "£1,500m for both years" rendering to be a rounding artefact. Divisional figures are used for all order-book analysis; both readings preserved.

D5 — Europe and Middle East FY2025 margin improvement: stated versus recomputed basis points (both preserved). The FY2025 announcement text was rendered as a +340bps improvement. Recomputation from the disclosed inputs gives £38.8m ÷ £873.4m = 4.44% against £7.9m ÷ £835.1m = 0.95%, an improvement of +349bps. Resolution: the recomputed figure is used in this report and is labelled as derived; the issuer-stated figure is preserved here. The difference is immaterial to any conclusion and may reflect rounding in the issuer's own presentation.

D6 — FY2025 operating cash flow to free cash flow bridge does not tie (unresolved). £258.4m net cash inflow from operating activities less £77.5m net capital expenditure = £180.9m, against reported free cash flow of £175.9m — a residual of £5.0m. Equivalent residuals are £4.2m (H1 2026) and £0.2m (H1 2025). The reconciling line items were not obtained from the announcements opened. Resolution: none. The residual is disclosed and left unexplained; no adjustment has been made to any figure and no conclusion in this report depends on closing the gap.
